Methodology

How the Demand Prediction Pipeline Operates

A systematic four-stage engineering pipeline transforms raw smart meter sensor readings into actionable grid balancing predictions.

Stage 01

Multi-Sensor Ingestion

Captures 15 simultaneous inputs including active/reactive voltages, aggregate line currents, kitchen/laundry sub-metering, and rolling 24-hour baseline averages.

Stage 02

PCA Dimensionality Reduction

Applies Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to compress correlated electrical features into 12 orthogonal components, preserving over 95% of total data variance while preventing multicollinearity.

Stage 03

Regression Modeling

High-throughput linear regression fits the transformed orthogonal vectors to project continuous household active power requirements in kilowatts (kW).

Stage 04

Analytics & Load Shifting

Translates raw kW into real-time tariff cost estimates ($ / INR), carbon emission rates, appliance percentage shares, and automated load-shifting recommendations.

Appliance Telemetry

Understanding Sub-Metering Decomposition

The system partitions electrical usage across dedicated residential zones to isolate high-drain equipment.

Sub-Metering 1: Kitchen Zone

Active Watt-Hours (Wh)

Tracks electrical energy consumed by kitchen equipment including dishwashers, microwave ovens, electric stoves, and refrigerators.

  • High intermittent draw spikes during meal preparation
  • Strongly correlated with morning (8:00 AM) and evening (7:00 PM) hours

Sub-Metering 2: Laundry Zone

Active Watt-Hours (Wh)

Measures energy consumed by washing machines, tumble dryers, and auxiliary laundry room heating elements.

  • Highest concentration on weekends and scheduled cleaning periods
  • Prime candidate for off-peak tariff rescheduling

Sub-Metering 3: Climate & Heating

Active Watt-Hours (Wh)

Monitors heavy thermal utilities including central air conditioning units, heat pumps, and electric water boilers.

  • Constitutes the largest continuous base load in extreme seasons
  • Directly influences peak grid stress during evening tariff windows (6–10 PM)
Data & Validation

Dataset & Machine Learning Architecture

Trained on real-world residential smart meter telemetry with rigorous preprocessing and dimensionality reduction.

Dataset Specifications

Data Source: UCI Individual Household Electric Power Consumption
Sampling Window: Minute-by-minute electrical telemetry over 47 months
Input Features: 15 Raw Physical & Temporal Telemetry Parameters
Reduced Components: 12 Orthogonal Principal Components (PCA)
Target Variable: Global Active Power (kW)

Preprocessing Pipeline

1
Median Imputation (SimpleImputer)

Replaces missing sensor telemetry with column-wise historical medians.

2
Standard Scaling (StandardScaler)

Normalizes features to zero mean and unit variance for uniform PCA weighting.

3
Linear Regression Estimator

Produces low-latency continuous predictions ideal for edge smart meters.
